\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Prompt-Level Defence is a Slope, and Sometimes Not Even That}
\label{sec:disc:slope}

The persona sweep's monotone reduction is tempting to read as a defence
working; the ladder complicates that reading, since two of ten rungs
performed no better than the unguarded baseline. B1 is the instructive
case, because its mechanism is structural: a system-prompt rule that
names the protected value and forbids its disclosure must place that
value in the model's context in order to state the rule, so it
simultaneously makes disclosure possible and supplies an explicit rule
for an override prompt to target. The rule is the vulnerability's
precondition and the attacker's map --- which is why M-01 is
architectural rather than a stronger prompt. The general form is that a
prompt-level control shifts a distribution; it does not establish a
boundary. For a control whose failures are silent and whose per-attempt
cost to an attacker is negligible, an $8.3\,\%$ residual is not residual
risk --- it is a certainty under repetition.

\subsection{Framing Beats Force: Refusal Tracks Surface Form}
\label{sec:disc:framing}

Table~\ref{tab:technique} has the widest applicability beyond this
target. Success concentrated in the two techniques that never ask for
the protected value in a recognisable form. Fictional framing works
because the persona instruction constrains how the assistant behaves
\emph{as an assistant} and says nothing about the content of a creative
artefact it has been asked to author; indirection works by explicitly
disclaiming the goal it achieves. The techniques that name their intent
most plainly were the weakest --- exactly the phrasings that refusal
training and keyword lists are built around. The ladder confirms this
from the other side, since B7's keyword gate was defeated by a
paraphrase and a hypothetical, and Table~\ref{tab:sysprompt} sharpens
it: a defence tuned against requests for the asset is not a defence
against requests for the policy protecting it.

\subsection{The Failing Layer, Not the Strength of the Defence}
\label{sec:disc:layers}

Holding the corpus fixed across ten configurations isolates which layer
fails, and each fails characteristically (Fig.~\ref{fig:layers}).

  \centering
  \safefig[0.56]{../figures/generated/fig-defence-layers.pdf}
  \caption{Where each defence sits in the pipeline, the technique class
  that defeats it, and its measured bypass rate.}
  \label{fig:layers}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Input filters fail by construction, and their cost is
availability.} The server decodes base64 and ROT13 before the model sees
them, so a literal scanner inspects a representation that no longer
exists downstream; the ASCII gate is defeated by pure-ASCII Spanish,
which is to say that language restriction is not a security control; and
the keyword gate is defeated by paraphrase, paying for its leak rate by
rejecting more than half of all traffic.

\textbf{Output filters convert a full disclosure into a partial one and
push the leak into another representation.} B5 and B9 scored zero on
literal leaks, which in isolation reads as success, yet $12$ of $97$
leaks study-wide were caught only by the obfuscation-aware branches. A
single-representation filter is defeated by reformatting a human reader
parses without effort; the control is to normalise before matching, or
preferably to remove the secret from scope.

\textbf{An untrusted data channel becomes an instruction channel by
default.} Content decoded from an uploaded image is concatenated into
the prompt with the same authority as typed input, and no model-level
defence addresses this, because from the model's position the two are
indistinguishable --- the absent data/instruction boundary Greshake et
al.\ identify as the root of indirect
injection~\cite[Sec.~2]{greshake2023}, reached here through the hidden
multi-modal delivery method of their
taxonomy~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{greshake2023}. The control therefore has to sit
at the application boundary, where that boundary can actually be drawn:
quote extracted content to the model as data, and apply the same input
policy to decoded content as to typed content.

\textbf{Agency converts a text failure into a data breach.} The single
cross-tenant SQL hit would be dismissed as noise on rate alone. It
should not be: the routing flag exists only in one row of an isolated
per-user table and cannot be guessed, so its appearance means the
application executed a model-authored query the caller was not entitled
to run. What sets the severity of an LLM01 failure is what the
surrounding system does with the output, not how often the model can be
induced to produce it --- which also makes
\code{Tool\bk Execution\bk Induced} scoring $0$ on the QR surface a
meaningful negative result.

\subsection{What the Detectors Disagree About}
\label{sec:disc:detectors}

Table~\ref{tab:agreement} is the methodological core of this study, and
it cuts in two directions.

\textbf{Generic detectors over-approximate by design.} Perfect recall at
$0.293$ precision is not a defect. The stock detector answers ``this
output is not a refusal'', a question that generalises across
deployments precisely because it is indifferent to any deployment's
policy; on this corpus most outputs are not refusals, including entirely
appropriate ones. A generic detector is a triage instrument: sound as a
filter, unsound as a verdict. The policy-aware detector can exist here
only because PwnzzAI's policy is legible from its source.

\textbf{Application-supplied oracles vary in trustworthiness across
surfaces of the same application}, from perfect agreement on the
catering RAG to a $76\,\%$ false-alarm rate on the order assistant. A
security signal shipped with the system is a hypothesis about the
system's behaviour, not a measurement of it, and cross-tabulating the
oracle rather than consuming it is what makes that testable.

\textbf{The policy-aware detectors are not exempt.} The catering
residual is the clearest case: with the mitigation on, no untrusted
passage was retrieved on any attempt, yet the term-presence detector
scored $0.417$, four of five hits being the model echoing a noun from
the question --- once inside an explicit refusal. Such a detector cannot
distinguish \emph{asserting} the injected policy from \emph{repeating
the querent's vocabulary while declining}. The second class is
\code{CouponLeak}'s whitespace-stripped branch, which matches without
word boundaries: it correctly caught a leak rendered as the plural
``Mushrooms!'' and also fired on an invented word containing the level's
secret. The rate-level impact is negligible; the methodological point is
not. Ground-truth detection removes the judgement call about \emph{what
counts as} a violation but not the engineering problem of
\emph{recognising} one, and both refinements that make these detectors
sharp buy sensitivity by spending precision. Recording the matched
rendering per hit is what makes the trade auditable after the fact.

\textbf{The judge is read the same way.} Its calibration
(Table~\ref{tab:judge}) is reported so that it is treated as an
instrument with a known profile rather than an oracle: at $65$--$68\,\%$
agreement and roughly half the real hits missed, its counts are not a
rate, and only its \emph{disagreements} with the ground-truth detectors
carry information. An instrument whose verdicts cannot be checked
against a constant must be characterised before it is believed.

\subsection{Poisoning: the Threshold is Not Where the Effect Begins}
\label{sec:disc:poisoning}

The sentiment results are the study's only exact measurements and
support three claims. \textbf{The attack is cheap}: five mislabelled
comments in a corpus consisting of the application's own comment table
plus the injection, so any pipeline retraining on user feedback without
provenance or review is exposed at this scale. \textbf{Output monitoring
is blind before the threshold}: every label-level monitor reports ``no
effect'' across the region in which the attack is progressing, whereas a
score-distribution monitor sees drift from the first injected comment.
\textbf{The backdoor is targeted, which is what defeats accuracy
testing}: behaviour is unchanged except on inputs containing a phrase
the attacker chose, so a poisoned model passes a held-out accuracy
check. The controls that can find it are provenance on training data,
label-distribution anomaly detection before retraining, and an
unpoisoned holdout monitored for drift on trigger-candidate terms.

We measure a threshold in a small deterministic classifier, whereas
Bowen et al.\ measure continuous learned-vulnerability curves in
instruction-tuned LLMs at poisoning rates of
$0.5$--$2\,\%$~\cite[Sec.~3]{bowen2025scaling} and find susceptibility
rising significantly with model scale~\cite[Table~2]{bowen2025scaling}.
The two agree that a very small fraction of attacker-controlled training
data suffices, and together they sharpen this study's limitation: our
absolute rates sit at the small end of a scale trend that runs the wrong
way for defenders.

For retrieval poisoning the picture is sharper than the headline rate
suggests. Unmitigated, influence was total, with two thirds of the hits
on queries that never named the injected terms; mitigated, untrusted
retrieval went to zero. Trust-tagged retrieval is therefore genuinely
effective at the stage it operates on, and the honest residual concern
is not that it leaks poison but that term-presence measurement cannot
certify that an answer is grounded in trusted context --- which needs an
output-grounding check, a different control at a different stage.

The recommendations share a shape. Every control that held acts on the
application pipeline --- what enters the context, what the retriever will
surface, what the tool layer may execute, what leaves the process. Every
control that failed acts on the model's disposition: a firmer
instruction, a keyword list, a string replacement on the way out. The
model is the component whose behaviour cannot be constrained by
assertion, and the pipeline is where the enforceable boundaries are.

Being Garak-native had three further consequences. It made the scan
auditable by construction, since every outcome traces to a Garak
evaluation entry in a retained \code{report.jsonl} and per-task
configurations replay any measurement through the stock CLI. It made the
comparison of detection philosophies free, because Garak already
supports extended detectors alongside a primary one --- which is also
what let the judge be added later as an ordinary detector rather than a
separate pipeline. And it disciplined the scope of custom code.

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:disc:limitations}

\textbf{Model scale bounds every absolute rate.} All LLM-mediated
measurements used a 1B model that refuses and drifts off-task more than
a production-scale one, so every absolute rate is a lower bound for this
deployment, not a property of the technique; the relative comparisons
hold the model fixed and are the intended contribution. For poisoning
the limitation has a known direction: susceptibility rises with
parameter count~\cite[Table~2]{bowen2025scaling}, so a larger deployment
should not be assumed safer.

\textbf{The sample per cell is small.} Three generations per prompt give
33--36 attempts per task, so one hit moves a task-level rate by roughly
three points; differences of one or two points between adjacent stages
are not resolvable and are not interpreted. The claims drawn are ordinal
and large-margin ($14/15$ vs $1/15$; $1.000$ vs $0.417$; $0.467$ vs
$0.083$), and the deterministic poisoning surface is exempt.

\textbf{Coverage is not a claim.} Each probe carries five to twelve
prompts chosen for attributability rather than breadth, so $0.000$ means
``not achieved by these prompts''; B6 and
\code{Tool\bk Execution\bk Induced} are explicitly of that kind.
\textbf{Detector precision is spent to buy sensitivity}, as
Sec.~\ref{sec:disc:detectors} sets out. \textbf{The judge is not a
measurement instrument here}: opt-in, calibrated only on a 46-attempt
sample against a 1B grader sharing weights with the target, and no rate
in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} depends on it. \textbf{Non-determinism is
bounded, not eliminated}, since the model behind the HTTP path is not
seedable through the application interface, so rates are stable in
ordering and approximate magnitude, not in their third decimal.
\textbf{And this is a single target in a single deployment}: the
transferable contributions are the method --- ground-truth detection
against a legible policy, paired controls, cross-tabulated oracles,
layer-isolating sweeps, a calibrated rather than trusted judge --- and
the technique-level orderings, not the rates.
