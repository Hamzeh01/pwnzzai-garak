\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The Failing Layer, Not the Strength of the Defence}
\label{sec:disc:layers}

\textbf{A prompt-level control shifts a distribution; it does not
establish a boundary.} B1's failure is structural rather than
accidental: a system-prompt rule that names the protected value and
forbids its disclosure must place that value in context in order to
state the rule, so the rule is at once the vulnerability's precondition
and the attacker's map --- which is why M-01 is architectural rather
than a stronger prompt. Where failures are silent and the per-attempt
cost to an attacker is negligible, an $8.3\,\%$ residual is not
residual risk but a certainty under repetition.

\textbf{Refusal tracks surface form, not effect.} Success concentrated
in the two techniques that never ask for the protected value in a
recognisable form: fictional framing works because the persona
instruction constrains how the assistant behaves \emph{as an assistant}
and says nothing about a creative artefact it is asked to author, and
indirection works by explicitly disclaiming the goal it achieves. The
phrasings refusal training and keyword lists are built around were the
weakest. The system-prompt probe generalises this: a defence tuned
against requests for the asset is not a defence against requests for
the policy protecting it.

\textbf{Input filters fail by construction, and pay in availability,
while output filters only relocate the leak.} A literal scanner
inspects a representation that no longer exists downstream once the
server has decoded it; language restriction is not a security control;
a keyword gate falls to paraphrase and charges more than half of all
traffic for it. A single-representation output filter is defeated by
reformatting a human reader parses without effort, so the control is to
normalise before matching, or better to remove the secret from scope.

\textbf{An untrusted data channel becomes an instruction channel by
default.} Content decoded from an uploaded image is concatenated into
the prompt with the same authority as typed input, and no model-level
defence addresses this, because from the model's position the two are
indistinguishable --- the absent data/instruction boundary Greshake et
al.\ identify as the root of indirect
injection~\cite[Sec.~2]{greshake2023}, reached through the hidden
multi-modal delivery method of their
taxonomy~\cite[Sec.~3.1]{greshake2023}. The control has to sit at the
application boundary, where the boundary can be drawn: quote extracted
content to the model as data, and apply the same input policy to
decoded content as to typed content. \textbf{And agency converts a text
failure into a data breach.} The single cross-tenant SQL hit would be
dismissed as noise on rate alone, but the routing flag exists only in
one row of an isolated per-user table and cannot be guessed, so its
appearance means the application executed a model-authored query the
caller was not entitled to run. What sets the severity of an LLM01
failure is what the surrounding system does with the output, not how
often the model can be induced to produce it --- which also makes
\code{Tool\bk Execution\bk Induced} scoring $0$ on the QR surface a
meaningful negative result.

\subsection{What the Detectors Disagree About}
\label{sec:disc:detectors}

The paired agreement figures of Sec.~\ref{sec:res:agreement} are the
methodological core of this study, and they cut in two directions.
\textbf{Generic detectors over-approximate by design:} perfect recall
at $0.293$ precision is not a defect. ``This output is not a refusal''
generalises across deployments precisely because it is indifferent to
any deployment's policy, so a generic detector is a triage instrument
--- sound as a filter, unsound as a verdict --- and the policy-aware
detector can exist here only because PwnzzAI's policy is legible from
its source. \textbf{Application-supplied oracles vary in
trustworthiness across surfaces of the same application:} a security
signal shipped with the system is a hypothesis about its behaviour, not
a measurement of it, and cross-tabulating the oracle rather than
consuming it is what makes that testable.

\textbf{The policy-aware detectors are not exempt.} The catering
residual is the clearest case: a term-presence detector cannot
distinguish \emph{asserting} the injected policy from \emph{repeating
the querent's vocabulary while declining}, which is what four of its
five residual hits are. The second class is \code{CouponLeak}'s
whitespace-stripped branch, which matches without word boundaries: it
correctly caught a leak rendered as the plural ``Mushrooms!'' and also
fired on an invented word containing the level's secret. The
rate-level impact is negligible; the methodological point is not.
Ground-truth detection removes the judgement call about \emph{what
counts as} a violation but not the engineering problem of
\emph{recognising} one, and both refinements that make these detectors
sharp buy sensitivity by spending precision --- recording the matched
rendering per hit is what makes the trade auditable. \textbf{The judge
is read the same way:} its calibration (Sec.~\ref{sec:arch:judge}) is
reported so it is treated as an instrument with a known profile rather
than an oracle, since at $65$--$68\,\%$ agreement with roughly half the
real hits missed its counts are not a rate, and only its
\emph{disagreements} carry information.

\subsection{Poisoning: the Threshold is Not Where the Effect Begins}
\label{sec:disc:poisoning}

The dose-response has an operational reading. Because the attack is
cheap and the backdoor targeted, a poisoned model passes a held-out
accuracy check; and because the label flip lags the probability drift,
every label-level monitor reports ``no effect'' across the region in
which the attack is progressing. The controls that can find it are
therefore provenance on training data, label-distribution anomaly
detection before retraining, and an unpoisoned holdout monitored for
drift on trigger-candidate terms --- scores, not labels. We measure a
threshold in a small deterministic classifier, whereas Bowen et al.\
measure continuous learned-vulnerability curves in instruction-tuned
LLMs at poisoning rates of $0.5$--$2\,\%$~\cite[Sec.~3]{bowen2025scaling}
and find susceptibility rising significantly with model
scale~\cite[Table~2]{bowen2025scaling}; both agree that a very small
fraction of attacker-controlled training data suffices.

For retrieval poisoning the picture is sharper than the headline rate
suggests: trust-tagged retrieval is genuinely effective at the stage it
operates on, and the honest residual concern is not that it leaks
poison but that term-presence measurement cannot certify that an answer
is grounded in trusted context --- which needs an output-grounding
check, a different control at a different stage.

The recommendations share a shape. Every control that held acts on the
application pipeline --- what enters the context, what the retriever
will surface, what the tool layer may execute, what leaves the process.
Every control that failed acts on the model's disposition: a firmer
instruction, a keyword list, a string replacement on the way out. The
model is the component whose behaviour cannot be constrained by
assertion, and the pipeline is where the enforceable boundaries are.
Being Garak-native had three further consequences: the scan is
auditable by construction, since every outcome traces to a Garak
evaluation entry in a retained \code{report.jsonl} and per-task
configurations replay any measurement through the stock CLI; comparing
detection philosophies was free, because Garak already supports
extended detectors alongside a primary one, which is also what let the
judge be added later as an ordinary detector rather than a separate
pipeline; and the scope of custom code stayed disciplined.

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:disc:limitations}

\textbf{Model scale bounds every absolute rate.} All LLM-mediated
measurements used a 1B model that refuses and drifts off-task more than
a production-scale one, so every absolute rate is a lower bound for
this deployment, not a property of the technique. For poisoning the
limitation has a known direction: susceptibility rises with parameter
count~\cite[Table~2]{bowen2025scaling}, so a larger deployment should
not be assumed safer. \textbf{The sample per cell is small}: three
generations per prompt give 33--36 attempts per task, so one hit moves
a task-level rate by roughly three points, and differences of one or
two points between adjacent stages are not resolvable and are not
interpreted. The claims drawn are ordinal and large-margin ($14/15$ vs
$1/15$; $1.000$ vs $0.417$; $0.467$ vs $0.083$), and the deterministic
poisoning surface is exempt. \textbf{Coverage is not a claim}: each
probe carries five to twelve prompts chosen for attributability rather
than breadth, so $0.000$ means ``not achieved by these prompts'', as
B6 and \code{Tool\bk Execution\bk Induced} explicitly are.
\textbf{Detector precision is spent to buy sensitivity}, as
Sec.~\ref{sec:disc:detectors} sets out, and \textbf{the judge is not a
measurement instrument here}: opt-in, calibrated only on a 46-attempt
sample against a 1B grader sharing weights with the target, and no
reported rate depends on it. \textbf{Non-determinism is bounded, not
eliminated}, since the model behind the HTTP path is not seedable
through the application interface, so rates are stable in ordering and
approximate magnitude, not in their third decimal. \textbf{And this is
a single target in a single deployment}: what transfers is the method
--- ground-truth detection against a legible policy, paired controls,
cross-tabulated oracles, layer-isolating sweeps, a calibrated rather
than trusted judge --- and the technique-level orderings, not the
rates.
