\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Prompt Injection, Direct and Indirect}
\label{sec:rel:injection}

Direct prompt injection --- overriding a system instruction from the
user turn --- was characterised early by Perez and
Ribeiro~\cite{perez2022ignore}, and the taxonomy of jailbreak techniques
has since been mapped both analytically~\cite{wei2023jailbroken} and
empirically from prompts collected in the wild~\cite{shen2024dan}.
Automated search methods produce transferable adversarial
suffixes~\cite{zou2023universal}. This literature is model-centric by
construction: its unit of analysis is the model's refusal behaviour, and
its notion of success is that the model said something it should not.

Greshake et al.~\cite{greshake2023} shifted the frame to
\emph{indirect} injection, where the adversarial instruction arrives
through a data channel the application itself opens --- a retrieved
document, a fetched page, an attachment --- and demonstrated end-to-end
compromise of LLM-integrated applications. Multi-modal variants smuggle
instructions through images and audio~\cite{bagdasaryan2023abusing},
which is the class our QR upload surface instantiates: the payload is
never typed, and the application's own decoder is the delivery mechanism.
Liu et al.~\cite{liu2024formalizing} give a formalisation and benchmark
of injection attacks and defences, and BIPIA~\cite{yi2023bipia}
benchmarks indirect injection specifically.

Proposed defences separate along the boundary this paper's results
follow. Prompt-level and filter-level controls --- delimiters,
instruction reminders, keyword gates --- are known to be
brittle~\cite{liu2024formalizing}. Controls that change the
\emph{architecture} rather than the phrasing fare better: spotlighting
marks untrusted spans so the model can be trained to discount
them~\cite{hines2024spotlighting}, StruQ separates instruction and data
channels structurally~\cite{chen2024struq}, instruction
hierarchies train a priority ordering over
sources~\cite{wallace2024hierarchy}, and the dual-LLM pattern denies the
privileged model any contact with untrusted text at
all~\cite{willison2023dual}. Our guardrail-ladder results
(Section~\ref{sec:res:ladder}) are an application-level measurement of
the first group's brittleness under a fixed attack corpus, and the
ablation in Section~\ref{sec:ablation} quantifies each layer's marginal
contribution.

\subsection{Red-Teaming Frameworks and Benchmarks}
\label{sec:rel:frameworks}

Automated red teaming with model-generated attacks was introduced by
Perez et al.~\cite{perez2022redteaming}, and Ganguli et
al.~\cite{ganguli2022redteaming} report the methods and scaling
behaviour of a large manual campaign. Standardised evaluation followed:
HarmBench~\cite{mazeika2024harmbench} fixes behaviours and a judge so
that attack methods can be compared, and
AgentDojo~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo} supplies a dynamic environment
in which prompt-injection attacks and defences are evaluated against
tool-using agents with checkable task outcomes. Tooling in the same space
includes Garak~\cite{garak}, a plugin-based scanner organised around
probes, generators, detectors and buffs, and PyRIT~\cite{pyrit}.

Two distinctions place the present work. First, benchmarks fix the target
so that methods are comparable; an assessment fixes the method so that
one deployment is characterised --- and the artefact it must produce is
not a leaderboard position but an auditable, replayable record for the
team that owns the system. Second, and more concretely, these frameworks
take a model or an agent as the object under test. We take the HTTP
surface of a conventional web application as the object under test, which
is possible in Garak without modification because a generator is defined
by its interface rather than by being a model. Where AgentDojo builds a
purpose-made environment with checkable outcomes, we derive checkable
outcomes from an existing application's own constants.

\subsection{Disclosure, Poisoning, and Retrieval Corruption}
\label{sec:rel:poisoning}

Memorised training data can be extracted from language models
verbatim~\cite{carlini2021extracting}, which establishes disclosure as a
model-level risk. The disclosure surfaces measured here are different in
kind: the personally identifying data is retrieved, not memorised, and
the leak is licensed by a system prompt that instructs the assistant to
provide customer details on request. The failure is a configuration
decision, and the analytically interesting result
(Section~\ref{sec:res:disclosure}) is that the requests which succeed are
the ones that look routine.

Backdoor poisoning of classifiers is long
established~\cite{gu2017badnets}, including in the concealed,
trigger-keyed form that leaves aggregate accuracy
untouched~\cite{wallace2021concealed}; Carlini et
al.~\cite{carlini2024poisoning} show that poisoning real web-scale
corpora is practical. Our sentiment surface reproduces the concealed
variety at the scale an application actually exposes --- a comment form
feeding a retraining step --- and, because the classifier is
deterministic, measures the dose-response exactly rather than
statistically. For retrieval-augmented generation~\cite{lewis2020rag},
PoisonedRAG~\cite{zou2025poisonedrag} shows that a small number of
injected passages can control a system's answers. We measure the same
attack with the application's trusted-only retrieval mitigation toggled,
and separate the retrieval-level effect from the answer-level signal ---
a separation that turns out to change the conclusion
(Section~\ref{sec:disc:detectors}).

\subsection{Taxonomies and Reporting Frames}
\label{sec:rel:taxonomy}

Findings here are grouped by OWASP LLM Top~10
categories~\cite{owasp2025}, which is the frame most likely to be
actionable for a team operating an application. NIST's adversarial
machine learning taxonomy~\cite{nist2024aml} and MITRE
ATLAS~\cite{atlas} provide the broader vocabulary for the poisoning and
agency findings. We use these as reporting frames, not as coverage
claims: the suite exercises three OWASP categories, and
Section~\ref{sec:disc:limitations} is explicit that a rate of $0.000$ in
this study means ``not achieved by these prompts'', never ``not
achievable''.
